# Rebuttal: Defense

**1. Forty orders do not show a full-time income.** Survives only as a gap, not as proof of failure. The record is silent on timing, price and margin, so it cannot show the shop is too small any more than it can show it is big enough; 40 paying orders are real demand.

**2. Nobody has worked out how long $8,000 lasts.** Partly survives. The record gives no monthly costs, so runway is unknown, but $8,000 was "set aside" for this purpose, which shows the user planned for a cushion rather than leaping with nothing.

**3. Quitting comes first.** Weakened. The claim that the 40 orders "came while the user was still employed" is not in the record; it is the prosecution's inference. Nor does the record say the job could be regained easily or not, so the cost of quitting is asserted, not shown.

**4. No evidence of repeat buyers or growth.** Does not survive as a reason to fail. "Friends and family" is speculation; the record says nothing about who bought. Silence on repeat customers is missing data, not evidence of flat sales.

**The point the jury should decide on:** Point 2. Runway is the one question arithmetic can settle: if $8,000 covers living and business costs long enough for 40 orders to grow, the plan works; the record does not supply monthly costs, so the jury must weigh whether that gap alone is fatal.
