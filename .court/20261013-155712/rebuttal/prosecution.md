# Rebuttal: Prosecution

1. **Paying customers: survives only in part.** Forty orders show someone will buy these candles. But the record gives no time period, prices or repeat-customer data. So the 40 orders show demand exists, not that it is enough to replace a salary.

2. **Cash cushion: does not survive as a strength.** The $8,000 exists, but its value depends on monthly costs, and the record has none. If no one can show it is too little, no one can show it is enough either. A cushion of unknown length cannot carry a plan to give up a known income.

3. **Same product, same channel: does not survive.** It assumes sales grow with hours worked, and the record shows no growth trend, sales rate or margin. "The most direct way to find out" is also the most expensive way, because it costs the job first. The same test can be run before quitting.

4. **The gap hurts both sides equally: does not survive.** The charge is about this plan *as stated*. A plan to quit a job with no prices, margins, costs, runway or salary comparison is not worked out. The missing numbers are exactly what the decision depends on. Their absence is a defect in the plan itself, not a tie.

**The point for the jury:** Can anyone, using only this record, show that 40 orders and $8,000 will cover the user's living costs once the salary stops? The defense concedes they cannot. That concession decides it.
