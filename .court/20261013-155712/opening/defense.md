# Opening Statement: Defense

The charge says this plan should not go ahead as described. The record does not support that. It shows a plan that already has buyers and money set aside to cover the move.

1. **People have already paid for the product.** The record says: "Sales so far: 40 orders." The plan is not starting from zero. Forty separate purchases show that buyers will pay for these candles on Etsy. The objection is that 40 is a small number. But the charge is about whether to start at all, and the hardest early hurdle, getting paying customers, has already been cleared forty times.

2. **There is a cash cushion set aside for this.** The record says: "Savings set aside: $8,000." The words "set aside" mean the user saved this money for the move ahead of time. The objection is that $8,000 may not last long. The record gives no monthly costs, so no one can show it is too little. What the record does show is a cushion that exists and was saved for this purpose.

3. **The product and the sales channel already work together.** The plan is to "sell handmade candles on Etsy full time." That is the same product on the same channel that produced the 40 orders. Going full time means doing more of something that has already sold. It does not mean switching to an untested idea. The objection is that part-time results may not grow. But putting more hours into a channel that already sells is the most direct way to find out.

4. **What I concede.** The record has no prices, margins, monthly costs, time period for the 40 orders, repeat-customer data, or current salary. Without these, I cannot show that the business will cover the user's living costs, or how long $8,000 will last. This gap hurts the defense. It hurts the prosecution equally, because nothing in the record shows the plan will fail. What the record does show is real paying customers and savings set aside for the move.
