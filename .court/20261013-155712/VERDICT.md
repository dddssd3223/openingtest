**The charge:** The case fails as stated: it should not go ahead the way it is described.

## Charges that stuck

1. **Forty orders show demand, not a living.** With no price, margin or time period, nobody can tell salary from hobby (11 jurors).
2. **Nobody knows how long $8,000 lasts.** No monthly costs were given, so it could be two months or twelve (10 jurors).
3. **The salary goes first.** The least reversible step comes before the shop proves it can pay the bills (8 jurors).

## What the defense got right

1. Forty paying orders prove real buyers exist.
2. The $8,000 was set aside on purpose, so the user planned a cushion.

## Conditions for acquittal

1. **Write down the monthly burn.** Add living costs to wax, shipping and Etsy fees, then divide $8,000 by the shortfall to get runway in months.
2. **Date and price the 40 orders.** From Etsy's records, work out profit per order, orders per month over a stated recent period and repeat buyers.
3. **Set a quit trigger.** Keep the job and name the monthly profit that must hold for several months before you resign. Add liability cover and business and tax registration to that checklist.

## The sentence

The court does not find the dream foolish; it finds the plan unfinished. Keep your job for now. This week, turn 40 orders and $8,000 into two numbers: monthly profit and months of runway. If the arithmetic holds, quit with a date and a safety net. If not, it cost you an evening, not a paycheck.
